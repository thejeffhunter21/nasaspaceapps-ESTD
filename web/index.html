Are two regions really warming at different rates?

One region being significant and another not does not prove they differ. This runs the direct test: a seasonal Mann-Kendall test on the monthly difference between the two regions.

Data quality

Where the measurements are solid

NASA's quality control drops a cell's month when clouds, smoke or sensor issues make the reading unreliable. Those months stay missing here; nothing is filled in. Most cells have nearly the full 264 months; a few have more gaps, which makes their individual trends less certain.

Earth Science Discovery Engine

The map shows what is happening. The AI investigates what is hidden beneath it.

On the server, an AI investigator works through every cell's 22-year record with controlled statistical tools: it forms a hypothesis, runs the tests, corrects for testing many cells at once, then tries to break its own result. Only findings that survive are marked statistically supported; everything else stays exploratory. The full cell dataset never leaves the server.

    How we tested it

    The seasonal Mann-Kendall test

    Phoenix nights swing by about 25 °C between winter and summer. A plain trend test sees that yearly wave and can miss a slow change hiding underneath it.

    The seasonal Mann-Kendall test never compares a July with a January. It compares each calendar month only with the same month in other years, July 2003 against July 2004, 2005 and so on, and counts how often later years are warmer. Then it adds up the evidence from all twelve months.

    The result is a direction (warming or cooling), a p-value (how likely a pattern this strong would be by chance alone; below 0.05 counts as significant), Kendall's τ (how consistent the rise is, from −1 to +1) and Sen's slope, the typical change per year, with a 95% confidence interval: the range of slopes the data support.

    What this can and can't tell you

    Surface, not air

    Satellites measure how warm the ground and rooftops are. That is not the air temperature a weather station or a person feels.

    Nighttime only

    These are nighttime passes. Hot nights matter because bodies need them to cool down, but daytime heat is a different measurement.

    A trend, not a cause

    The test shows that warming happened, not why. City growth and climate change are possible reasons that scientists study; we prove neither.

    Not a forecast

    Slopes describe 2003–2024. They do not predict next month or any future year.

    5 km cells

    Each column is a roughly 5 km square: neighbourhood-scale patterns, not individual streets.
