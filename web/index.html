How we tested it

Every number here comes from NASA satellite measurements and the checks below. Open a question for the short answer. "Technical detail" is there if you want the exact method.

What does the satellite measure?

How hot the ground, roofs and roads are: land surface temperature, read from their infrared glow by NASA's Terra satellite. It is not the air temperature a weather station reports.

Technical detail

MODIS on Terra, product MOD11C3 version 6.1: monthly land surface temperature on a 0.05° climate-modelling grid. We use the nighttime layer LST_Night_CMG (Terra's night pass is around 10:30 pm local time), converted from kelvin to °C.

Why nighttime?

Cities soak up heat by day and release it after dark. Night temperatures show how much heat Phoenix holds, and hot nights matter for health because bodies need to cool down.

Technical detail

Daytime surface temperature also exists in MOD11C3 (LST_Day_CMG) but is not part of this release.

How is Phoenix divided?

Into 192 grid cells of about 5 km, 16 across and 12 down, grouped into four quarters. The map shows the cells inside the Phoenix urban area; cells outside it are hidden on the map but still analysed.

Technical detail

Window 112.4°–111.6° W, 33.2°–33.8° N; quarters split at 112.0° W and 33.5° N. Cell ids are rRR_cCC with row 0 in the north. The urban outline is Natural Earth's MODIS-derived urban extent (c. 2002–2003), used for display only.

How are regional averages made?

For each month, the valid cells in a region are averaged. If less than 80% of a region passed quality control that month, the region's value is left blank.

Technical detail

Cosine-latitude weighted mean of the region's valid cells. The analysis service re-computes this from the cell file and refuses to run unless it reproduces the published regional series (tolerance 0.0001 °C).

What happens to missing measurements?

Clouds, smoke or sensor problems make some months unreliable. Those stay missing; nothing is filled in or estimated. An annual average is shown only when all 12 months are valid.

Technical detail

Kept only when NASA's quality flags are good and the estimated error is under 1 K (policy phoenix-qc-v1.0). 50,276 of 50,688 cell-months (99.2%) pass.

How is a warming trend calculated?

The seasonal Mann-Kendall test compares each calendar month only with the same month in other years (July with July), so the summer–winter cycle never counts as a trend. Sen's slope gives the typical change per year.

Technical detail

pymannkendall seasonal_test (period 12) on the monthly series with gaps left as missing; Sen's slope per month × 12 = per year. Phoenix nights swing by about 25 °C between winter and summer, which is why the seasonal version is needed.

When is a result "significant"?

When a pattern this strong would be very unlikely by chance (p below 0.05). Testing 192 cells at once would produce some false alarms, so cell results are corrected and reported as q-values.

Technical detail

Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate across all cells. AI findings are "statistically supported" only if significant after correction and the direction holds in both 2003–2013 and 2014–2024; otherwise "exploratory". Region-vs-region claims use a direct test on the monthly difference.

What does a 95% confidence interval mean?

The range of warming rates the data support. If the whole range is above zero, warming is clear. If two ranges overlap a lot, the difference may not be real.

Technical detail

Within-season pairwise slopes pooled; C = z·√Var(S) from the seasonal Mann-Kendall variance; bounds at ranks (N−C)/2 and (N+C)/2+1 (Gilbert 1987). Its median equals the published Sen's slope exactly (tested).

Missing data

Clouds, smoke or sensor problems sometimes make a reading unreliable. NASA flags those, and we leave them out. Those months stay empty here; nothing is filled in or guessed. Most squares have nearly all 264 months. A few have more gaps, so we are less sure about them.

To see one year at a time, switch the map to "Data gaps".

What this can't tell you
  • Surface, not air: not the temperature a person feels.
  • Nighttime only.
  • A trend, not a cause: city growth and climate change are possible reasons; we prove neither.
  • Not a forecast: slopes describe 2003–2024 only.
  • 5 km cells: neighbourhood patterns, not streets. Neighbouring cells are correlated, and month-to-month correlation makes p-values somewhat optimistic.
